# Entrepreneurship For All (Part I) - The UC Berkeley Startup Guide for students by Silicon Valley Insiders

This is a program on entrepreneurship, by entrepreneurs, for everyone. It gives students the Silicon Valley insiders’ view of startups, as its lecturers and guest speakers share their extensive experience in launching, growing, and funding successful start ups.

**The lecturer's proceeds from this course will be donated to nonprofits in the education space.**
Lectures, readings, discussions, and quizzes will cover core concepts along the startup life cycle:

> - VALIDATE: Idea and business validation (Why? Why Now? Why You?); Market sizing and analysis.
>- RECRUIT: Recruiting and incentivizing cofounders and advisors; attracting and retaining a great team. 
> - BUILD: User-centric design and efficient product development to Minimal Viable Product (MVP) launch and beyond.
> - FUND: Bootstrapping; accelerators and incubators; angel and seed financing; raising venture capital. 
> - SCALE: Applying Lean Startup Methodology to achieve Product-Market Fit; Growth hacking strategies for sales and customer acquisition; distribution partnerships; scaling to liquidity events (M&A and IPOs).

#### This program will give you the skills and confidence to:

> - start and help scale tech and non-tech startups
> - start or join social impact ventures (social entrepreneurship)
> - bring startup thinking and values to research and academic institutions, small- to-midsize enterprises (SMEs), and large corporations (intrapreneurship)

---
#### **Outcomes**
This course is designed to inspire and empower you to be a successful entrepreneur. By the end of this course, you will be able to:
- **Understand** and **evaluate** the different types of entrepreneurial models across tech/non-tech startups, social impact startups, and big company Intrapreneurship.
- **Use** the Lean Startup Method to design and launch user-centric products that are desirable, viable, and feasible, and continuously test your way to Product-Market Fit.
- **Build**, **motivate**,  and **grow** a great team and establish a vibrant startup culture.
- **Raise funding** for your startup: from angel and seed financing to raising venture capital and growth capital.
- **Acquire customers** using growth hacking techniques and strategic partnerships.
- **Scale** your startup to liquidity events via Mergers & Acquisition (M&A) and Initial Public Offerings (IPOs).

## Schedule & What are we going to cover and How?
Below we have outlined the course schedule over the next 8 weeks, demonstrating which modules and associated topics should be completed during which week.
#### **Week 1: The Many Faces of Entrepreneurship**
- Course Introduction
- The many types of entrepreneurship: tech and non-tech entrepreneurs, social entrepreneurs, non-profit entrepreneurs, and intrapreneurs
- The Entrepreneur as “Ethical Persuader.”
#### **Week 2: The Language & Life Cycle of Entrepreneurship I**
- Concepts and terminology along the life cycle of a startup: ideation, market analysis, incorporation, recruiting, bootstrapping, product development, fundraising, sales, scaling, and liquidity event.
#### **Week 3: The Language & Life Cycle of Entrepreneurship II**
- The language of entrepreneurship
- Continued concepts and terminology along the life cycle of a startup: ideation, market analysis, incorporation, recruiting, bootstrapping, product development, fundraising, sales, scaling, and liquidity event.
#### **Week 4: Persuading Yourself I:**
- The importance of clarity in the problem statements and of collaboration in solution discovery
- Establishing total and serviceable addressable market size for a product/service
- Introduction to design thinking as seeking the intersection of the feasible, the viable, and the desirable
- The concept of product-market fit with illustrative examples.
#### **Week 5: Persuading Yourself II:**
- An introduction to the lean startup methodology
- Lean startup principles as tools for idea evaluation and product development
- Defining and developing the MVP (Minimum Viable Product). Tools and approaches for wire-framing and prototyping. How to continuously test your way to product-market fit.
#### **Week 6: Persuading Team Members: Building the Organization**
- Crafting a startup’s mission beyond generic corporate mission statements. Establishing values and corporate culture
- Persuading co-founders and early employees (first concentric circle around the entrepreneur). A guide to founder equity and incorporation
- Social entrepreneurship and B corporations: incorporating social impact into the legal corporate structure.
#### **Week 7: Persuading Advisors**
- The role of advisors - is the second concentric circle about the entrepreneur. Finding, choosing, and persuading advisors
- Experienced entrepreneurs as advisors on marketing, sales, and business development strategy and execution. Managing expectations with advisors: time commitment, network access, equity stake. Finding an entrepreneur-centric legal counsel
- The good, the bad, and the ugly of the Board of Directors.
#### **Week 8: Persuading Investors: Initial Fundraising**
- A guide to initial fundraising and to bootstrapping
- Pre-seed, seed, angel, and venture investments
- Business summaries, presentations, and plans.
